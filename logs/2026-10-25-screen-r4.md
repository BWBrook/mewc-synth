Production-like VTL (ViT-L/16, 384 px), same screen recipe; A0 (pseudo-rare classes at 50 real
snips) against S20 (plus 200 look-alike translations per class at a 20% share). About 2.8 to 3.6 h
per run.

No class harmed under the amended rule. **Under the criterion registered on 24 October, the S20 gain
transfers to the production model**: the same size as on VTB (+0.029), with a small gain on the other
92 classes as well, and S20 also narrows the seed-to-seed spread (lowest seed 0.856 against 0.815).
The two-seed check of round 3 (+0.004) was a draw from that spread, not a different answer.

## Model-size pilot (in progress)

Every class at its real data (R composition, cap 4,000), screen recipe, two seeds per model (VTB three),
timed with `src/bench_infer.py` on the A100 (batch 64) and on 8 CPU cores. Seed-1 results so far:

| Model | Params | macro-F1, all | macro-F1, camera-trap | A100 img/s | 8-core CPU img/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VTL | 304 M | 0.949 | 0.921 | 108 | about 1 |
| VTB | 86 M | 0.937 | 0.909 | 186 | about 5 |
| ViT-S | 22 M | 0.930 | 0.898 | 308 | 12 |

The first pass of the EfficientNet and ConvNeXt runs failed at the first unfreeze: the screen script
passed `BUF=9` (blocks to unfreeze), right for ViTs, while these models expose three or four
blocks; mewc-train suggests 1 to 2 for them. Not a mewc-train fault (2.0.9 fails the same way).
`run_screen.sh` now takes `BUF`, and the eight convolutional models were relaunched on 25 October at
00:08 UTC with `BUF=2`, seed 1 for all first, then seed 2 where it fits before 11:00 UTC. The
8-core CPU timings above come from 32 images and vary up to twofold between machines; a 64-image
benchmark of every model follows the runs, and `src/pilot_summary.py` assembles the final table
(`screen/pilot_summary.csv`, copied to `deep-server:/data/mewc/screen_orch/out/`).
