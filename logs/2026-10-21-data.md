# 2026-10-21 Snip library: transfer, audit, grouping and splits

## Transfer and extraction

- Source `deep-server:/data/data_600px.zip` (196,354,586,754 bytes, 4,070,047
  entries, all stored). Pushed by rsync over the direct tailnet path at about
  100 MB/s, in 37 min with exit 0 (rsync verifies the whole file). An extra
  source `sha256sum` running at the same time thrashed `deep-server`'s HDD
  array (100% utilisation, 44 MB/s) and was stopped. Separate SHA-256 runs on
  both ends were restarted afterwards for the record.
- The first extractor opened the zip in 32 workers at about 6.4 GB each, ran
  ngx-gpu out of memory and needed an OpenStack hard reboot (see
  `nectar-gpu` log). Fixed: the parent parses the index once, and workers read
  stored entries by offset with a CRC-32 check.
- Working set (cap 30,000 per class, seed 20261021): 1,065,958 snips from 120
  classes (96 in the model); `snips_meta/class_table.csv` and
  `selection.parquet`. 591,413 from base, 462,033 from sufficient, 11,524
  from need_more and 988 from too_few.

## Audit

- Every image is 600 x 600 RGB except one `swamp_rat` snip at 578 x 600. The
  inventory has 1,065,957 files against 1,065,958 extracted; one entry
  probably collided on file name (not yet traced).
- JPEG quality: 980,883 at q75 (`mewc-snip`'s PIL default) and **85,074 at
  q95**, all from one source with UUID-style file names. That source is
  class-correlated: australian_fur_seal 98% q95, eastern_bettong 44%,
  superb_lyrebird 34%, black_currawong 33%, long_nosed_potoroo 33%,
  southern_brown_bandicoot 24%, cat 23%, bare_nosed_wombat 20%. This is a
  potential compression shortcut for any classifier. Synthetic snips will be
  saved at each class's empirical q75/q95 mix.
- Exact duplicates: 13,353 files share 6,644 hashes. **114 hashes carry two
  class labels** (231 files, excluded from all splits and listed in
  `splits/split_v1/excluded.csv`). The commonest pairs are brown quail /
  painted buttonquail (22), black rat / swamp rat (9), brown goshawk / swamp
  harrier (7), olive whistler / tasmanian scrubwren (5), black currawong /
  forest raven (4) and black rat / house mouse (4). Barry to adjudicate.
- Chroma is bimodal: 274,602 snips below 0.25 (greyscale IR), a thin
  valley from 0.25 to 2 (about 13k), and colour from about 3 upward. The IR
  cut-off is set at 1.0 (26.7% IR).

## Near-duplicate grouping

- DINOv2 nearest-neighbour similarity: median 0.849, 90th percentile 0.943,
  99th percentile 1.0.
- Visual check of nearest-neighbour pairs in bands 0.88-0.92, 0.92-0.95,
  0.95-0.97 and 0.97-0.99 (8 random pairs each): essentially all are the
  same event (burst frames: same animal, pose and background). Their pHash
  distances ranged from 0 to 34, so the hash adds little.
- At tau 0.90, chaining forms giant components: short_beaked_echidna 18,487,
  unknown_animal 9,166, peafowl 8,751, spotted_tail_quoll 8,618, plus 63
  groups of 200 or more covering 150,725 snips. A sweep gave a largest
  group of 10,545 at 0.94/8, 6,290 at 0.95/6, 3,182 at 0.96/6, 691 at 0.97/4
  and 83 at 0.98/2. Raising tau would split real bursts across splits
  (leakage). The giant components look like repeat visits to one camera,
  which is close to the site grouping we lack. **Kept tau 0.90 / Hamming 8.**
  Splits admit a group to test or validation only if it fits the remaining
  quota (+10), so giant groups stay in train.

## vGPU licensing broken by Tailscale DNS

After the hard reboot the vGPU ran unlicensed. `nvidia-gridd` could not
resolve `api.cls.licensing.nvidia.com`, because Tailscale (direct
`resolv.conf` mode) had pointed DNS at 100.100.100.100. Its
`resolv.pre-tailscale-backup.conf` then contained 100.100.100.100 itself, a
forwarding loop. Unlicensed vGPU runs at full speed for about 20 min, then
throttles: the embedding finished inside that window and grouping stalled
after it. Fixed with `tailscale set --accept-dns=false` and by restoring
`/etc/resolv.conf -> /run/systemd/resolve/stub-resolv.conf` (Nectar's
1.1.1.1 and 8.8.8.8 on eth0). The vGPU was licensed again immediately (lease
to 2026-10-22 05:04 UTC).

## Stage A v1 launched (05:16 UTC)

- Cache: up to 1,500 train snips per class across 120 classes, pseudo-rare
  classes at their n=50 subsets, IR cut-off 1.0, seed 7.
- LoRA rank 64, lr 1e-4, 10k steps, batch 32, alpha 0.5, min-SNR 5. Sample
  grids every 2k steps for six prompts: quoll IR and black currawong day
  (pseudo-rare), painted buttonquail day and pygmy possum IR (need_more),
  Tasmanian devil IR (abundant), and flame or scarlet robin day (merged).

## Stage A v1 progress (20:20 AEDT)

- Cache: 121,990 snips, 120 classes, 217 captions, 19.1% IR; built in 55 min.
  Training started 06:11 UTC at 20.7 snips/s, GPU 95 to 98%, 22.9 GB. The
  diffusion loss is flat and noisy (0.113 to 0.121 per 1,000-step mean), as
  usual for this objective.
- Sample grids at steps 2k, 4k and 6k
  (`docs/figures/2026-10-21-stage-a-v1-step{2000,6000}.jpg`):
  - The camera-trap look is learned: IR greyscale with eye-shine, motion
    blur, litter backgrounds, squared-snip framing, and the right body plan
    in every row. That is a large change from untuned SDXL.
  - Concerns for review:
    - Spotted-tailed quoll (pseudo-rare, 50 snips): the body spots are right,
      but the tails look plain or bushy, without the diagnostic tail spots.
      They may be borrowing eastern quoll.
    - Black currawong (pseudo-rare): one sample shows a greyer bird with a
      white undertail, which may be grey or pied currawong features.
    - Flame or scarlet robin: glossy perched-bird photographs.
- **Source mix explains the robins.** `flame_robin` is 98% `ebird`-prefixed
  snips. Overall 20.4% of snips are `ebird` (217k across 47 classes) and 18.5k
  are `inat`. 27 classes are more than half `ebird`, including the need_more
  classes scrubtit, strong_billed_honeyeater and tasmanian_boobook. The
  generator learns photo style for these classes. The production classifier
  learns them largely from photos yet is deployed on camera-trap snips.
- Proposed for Stage A v2: source-aware captions ("camera trap snip" vs
  "photograph"), so camera-trap-style snips can be requested for
  photo-dominated classes. Needs Barry to say which filename prefixes are
  non-camera-trap sources (likely `ebird`, `inat`).
