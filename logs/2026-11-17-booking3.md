## 19 November: Barry's review result (100 snips, blind)

Answers are in `ops/2026-11-19/rare_review_answers/` and scored in `rare_review_scored.csv`
(`rare_review_eval.py`). Barry gave the species answers first; the page recorded the real-or-synthetic answers
only once both were given, so he added those in a second pass.

- **Real or synthetic:** he called 24 of 50 translations synthetic (48%) and 12 of 50 real snips synthetic (24%),
  with no "unsure". That is above chance, but less separable than the pseudo-rare audit of 31 October (58%
  against 17%).
- **Species on translations:** 31 of 50 read as the intended class (62%; 30 of 48 on 31 October), 10 as the
  look-alike source, 7 unsure and 2 as neither (both brown hare translations he called rabbit).
- **Species on real snips:** 46 of 50 correct. The misses were scrubtit (called Tasmanian scrubwren), house mouse
  (black rat) and two unsure (swamp rat, pygmy possum).
- **Agreement with the VTL identity check:**
  - Both eastern rosella translations read as crimson rosella (VTL: 8% rosella).
  - One of two read as the source for chestnut teal (VTL 15%) and goat (VTL 7%).
  - He reads both starling translations as common blackbird where VTL accepts 60% as starling.
  - Brown hare reads as rabbit; VTL also gives 20% to European rabbit.
- **Notes worth acting on after the booking:**
  - He suspects some training snips labelled superb lyrebird are skinks (a misclick). That would explain why the
    classifiers' top confusion for skink is lyrebird. It calls for a label check of lyrebird training snips.
  - A real black rat snip is near-identical to about five others he saw, which fits the near-duplicates in the
    October audit.
  - Some pipit-like snips may be a Victorian species outside the class list.
