## mewc-torch

`src/mewc_torch/` reproduces the mewc-train 2.0.10 recipe on timm backbones (timm 1.0.30, torch 2.14):

- `data.py`: per-epoch class sampling (with replacement only for classes smaller than the sample),
  resize as `tf.image.resize` does (bilinear, no antialiasing), torchvision RandAugment with the
  0..1 magnitude mapped onto 31 bins, normalisation by the backbone's own mean and standard deviation.
- `model.py`: average-pooled timm features, then dropout, a compression layer sized by mewc-train's
  rule, ELU, dropout and the class layer. Blocks are unfrozen from the top using timm's coarse
  parameter groups, classified by content (`blocks`, `stages`, `layers`, `levels`) so that
  EfficientNet's head convolution, which timm groups with the last stage, counts with that stage.
  With two blocks: ConvNeXt stages 2 and 3 (95% of ConvNeXt-Atto's weights, the same partition as
  mewc-train's ConvNeXt), EfficientNet stages 4 and 5 plus head (as mewc-train), ViT the top
  transformer blocks plus the final norm. BatchNorm stays in inference mode, as in mewc-train.
- `train.py`: frozen stage on the initial sample (early stopping on training loss, best weights
  restored), then progressive stages with a fresh AdamW (AMSGrad) optimiser, exponential decay to a
  fifth over the run, dropout and magnitude stepped per stage, a fresh sample each epoch, best epoch by
  validation loss; categorical focal loss (alpha 0.25, gamma 2); bf16 autocast; any batch size; a
  checkpoint every epoch with resume.
- `predict.py` writes the same score files as `vtl_score.py`; `export.py` writes ONNX (raw 0..255 RGB
  in, probabilities out, dynamic batch) and checks it against PyTorch; `bench.py` times the ONNX model in
  `onnxruntime`.

CPU smoke tests on real snips (four classes, 30 training and 10 validation snips each), all passed:
ConvNeXt-Atto and ViT-Tiny train through both stages with the expected learning-rate path; a run
killed after epoch k = 3 resumed at k = 4 with continuous history and learning rate; ONNX matches
PyTorch within 4e-7; `onnxruntime` on 8 CPU threads reached 93 (ConvNeXt-Atto) and 57 (ViT-Tiny)
images/s at 224 px. kimm's ViT-B, ViT-L, ConvNeXt-B and ConvNeXt-S weights are the timm checkpoints
`vit_base_patch16_384.augreg_in21k_ft_in1k`, `vit_large_patch16_384.augreg_in21k_ft_in1k`,
`convnext_base.fb_in22k_ft_in1k` and `convnext_small.in12k_ft_in1k`, so the parity gate compares the
same starting weights.

## Data on the GPU volumes

`src/extract_v2.py` ran on the parked ngx-gpu-4 (c3.small, 2 vCPUs) straight from the zip on `ml-data`:
1,210,935 snips in about five minutes, no CRC failures, 55 GB, at `/mnt/ml-data/mewc-synth/v2/`
(`train/`, `val/`, `test/`, `test_proj/`, one list CSV each, `classes.txt`; 96 classes, 59 in the
held-out projects). Copied to ngx-gpu-5's `ml-data-2` over the project network. Both VMs' environments
now include timm and `onnxruntime`.

`src/run_v2.sh` runs one phase 1 job with either stack, scores the full validation set and appends a
row to `v2_runs/registry.jsonl` through `src/v2_eval.py` (macro-F1 over all classes, camera-trap snips
and the 25 low-count classes; accuracy; expected calibration error).
