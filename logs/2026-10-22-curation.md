## Embedding: CPU too slow, moved to the A100

`src/curate_embed.py` reads stored zip entries by offset (CRC-checked, no extraction) and matches
`embed.py` preprocessing (DINOv2-base CLS, bicubic 224, ImageNet normalisation, unit-normalised,
float16), so full-library embeddings are comparable with the working-set audit. Shards of 50,000
rows make it resumable.

CPU benchmark on deep-server (2 x Xeon E5-2683 v4, AVX2, 32 threads): 16.2 images/s, about 70 h for
the library. The A100 did the 1.07 M working set in 9 minutes, so the run is queued on ngx-gpu
behind the classifier screening (`curation/v1/curate_gpu.sh` waits for `QUEUE DONE`), using the
identical `index.parquet` pushed from deep-server. deep-server pulls the outputs back
(`curate_pull.sh`, polling every 5 minutes).

## Grouping

`src/curate_groups.py`: per class, exact all-pairs cosine on the GPU in 4,096-row chunks, upper-
triangle edges at cosine >= 0.90, connected components at 0.90 (the threshold calibrated visually on
21 October) and at 0.95. Per class: groups, singletons, share of snips in multi-snip groups, snips
retained at one or three per group, largest group, and nearest-neighbour cosine median and 90th
percentile. The full library has more frames per burst than the capped working set, so chaining
(giant components) is the main thing to check before any threshold is used for decisions.

## Results (A100 run, 22 October)

- Embedding: 4,069,864 snips in 61 minutes on ngx-gpu-4 (about 1,100 to 1,550 images/s, sharing
  the GPU with other jobs at times); 0 decode or CRC failures. Grouping: 1 minute. Outputs pulled to
  `deep-server:/data/mewc/curation/v1/` (shards 5.9 GB, `groups.parquet`, `redundancy_by_class.csv`).
- Whole library: 2,808,943 components at cosine 0.90 and 3,655,590 at 0.95.
- **Single-linkage chaining at full scale.** At 0.90 the largest components are 97,410 pademelons,
  74,998 possums, 65,826 Cape Barren geese and 44,387 Bennett's wallabies. The contact sheet of the
  pademelon giant shows different animals at different sites, day and night: a chain, not a burst.
  The next-largest 0.90 groups (172 to 882 snips) are genuine bursts (one animal, one camera,
  consecutive frames), and pairs just above 0.90 are consecutive frames of one event. So 0.90 is a
  good pairwise "same event" threshold, but connected components over-merge in large classes. At
  0.95 the largest components are 40 (pademelon), 418 (possum) and 4,125 (Cape Barren goose);
  pairs just above 0.95 are near-identical frames.
- Share removable by keeping one snip per 0.95 component: pademelon 6%, possum 8%, Bennett's wallaby
  13%, fallow deer 17%, Cape Barren goose 23%, echidna 32% (largest 0.95 component 6,228, a long
  static sequence).

Implications, for the decisions deferred until the synthetic work is done:

- For group-disjoint splits, 0.90 components err on the safe side (a chain only moves more snips
  together), though giant chains make test quotas lumpy; the working-set splits already keep them in
  train.
- For a parsimonious core, use chaining-free thinning instead of components: greedy selection in a
  random order, keeping a snip only if no kept snip is within the threshold (k-centre style, blockwise
  on the GPU). Counts per class at 0.90 and 0.95 are the next mechanical step.
