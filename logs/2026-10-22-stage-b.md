# 2026-10-22 Stage B round 1: contrastive per-class adapters

Each adapter continues from the Stage A v2 LoRA (all 1,120 tensors reloaded
exactly at model precision; cast to float32 before loading). It trains 800
steps (batch 16, lr 5e-5, warmup 50, min-SNR 5) on cached rows: target 45%
sampling mass, look-alike(s) 25%, and 30% spread over 3,000 random rows of
other classes against forgetting. Checkpoints at 400 and 800 steps.
Generation used seed 13, guidance 5, camera-trap captions, real IR/q95
shares, 64 target and 32 look-alike snips per checkpoint, judged by the
production ViT-L. Tables: `logs/2026-10-22-stage-b-r1-identity.csv` and
`logs/2026-10-22-stage-b-r1-memorisation.csv`. Sheet:
`docs/figures/2026-10-22-stage-b-nn.jpg`.

Memorisation and diversity (DINOv2; same-event bursts are at 0.90 or
more):

- **No copies.** 0% of synthetic target snips reach 0.90 to any training
  row.
- **Low diversity and anchoring.** Mean pairwise similarity is 0.83 for
  synthetic quolls against 0.46 to 0.52 for real test quolls, and 0.75 to
  0.78 for currawongs against 0.48. Synthetic quolls sit closer to the 50
  training snips (median nearest 0.82) than real held-out quolls do (0.72).
  The nearest-neighbour sheet shows **7 of 8 B800 quolls share the same
  nearest training snip**, an orange-tinted flash photo, and they copy its
  look rather than its pixels. Real test quolls are mostly IR, in varied
  poses, with partial bodies and cage frames.
- Scrubtit synthetic snips are farther from real scrubtits (0.66) than real
  scrubtits are from each other (0.80). With mostly eBird training snips and
  15 camera-trap ones, the adapter has not learned a camera-trap scrubtit.

Reading: the contrastive adapter fixes identity, as judged, for classes
with a close look-alike, but at n=50 it does so by anchoring on a few
training exemplars. As classifier training data such sets risk new
shortcuts (for example "orange tint means spotted-tailed quoll"). Next:
a diversity sweep with checkpoint 400 vs 800, guidance 2.5/3.5/5 and
viewpoint phrases, measuring identity, pairwise diversity and anchor share
together. The currawong look-alike damage needs rebalanced weights.

## Barry's review of the v2 camera-trap identity sheet (2026-10-22, chat, paraphrased)

- Synthetic boobooks look like brown falcons. Boobooks are mostly seen at
  night; by day they would be in trees only. So daytime ground-camera
  boobooks are ecologically implausible. Generation conditions must follow
  each species' real detection conditions, not a raw IR share (the camera-trap
  boobook snips were 50% daytime).
- Strong-billed honeyeaters generally look pretty good. Crescent and
  strong-billed can be hard to tell apart except by the head.
- Scrubtits: the ones the ViT-L called olive whistler just have colour a
  little wrong (too olive); the wing dot is diagnostic. Overall they look
  pretty good.

Reading: Barry rates the synthetic scrubtits and honeyeaters higher than the
ViT-L does. The judge is weakest where it matters most. It learned
scrubtits mostly from eBird photos, so camera-trap-style scrubtits may
default to Tasmanian scrubwren. Test queued: ViT-L accuracy on real snips of
these classes by source (photo against camera trap). Expert blind review
stays essential.

## Diversity sweep (finished 00:04 UTC)

Quoll and currawong adapters: checkpoint 400 or 800, crossed with guidance
2.5, 3.5 or 5, plus guidance 3.5 with a random viewpoint phrase ("close up",
"partial view", "rear view", "side view", "at a distance", "walking", "head
down foraging", "half out of frame"). 48 snips per cell, seed 21. Tables:
`logs/2026-10-22-stage-b-sweep-{identity,memorisation}.csv`.

- Identity holds across every cell: 0.96 to 1.00 intended.
- Pairwise similarity (lower is more diverse) is best at **400 steps,
  guidance 2.5**: quoll 0.733, currawong 0.678. At guidance 5 it is 0.70 to
  0.80. Real test snips: 0.44 to 0.51.
- Anchor share at 400/2.5 is 0.21 (currawong) and 0.25 (quoll), against
  0.31 to 0.40 at 800/5. Real held-out snips score 0.13 to 0.18 against the
  same 50 training rows, so anchoring is now near the natural level.
- Viewpoint phrases did not increase diversity (pairwise 0.76 to 0.80).
- Synthetic nearest-training similarity stays about 0.80, against 0.71 to
  0.74 for real test snips. Snips cluster around the training set rather
  than spanning the species' range.

Recipe so far: Stage B 400 steps, guidance 2.5. Prompt-side tweaks do not
close the diversity gap.

## Next: translation from look-alikes (Diff-Mix style)

`src/translate.py` runs SDXL img2img with the Stage B adapter on real
camera-trap train snips of the abundant look-alike (eastern quoll, grey
currawong, Tasmanian scrubwren), at strengths 0.5, 0.65 and 0.8. Each
source snip's own day/IR state sets the caption, at guidance 3.5. The aim is
to inherit real pose, framing, background and lighting diversity while the
adapter supplies the target's features. Measured with the same identity,
diversity and anchoring metrics.

Findings:

1. The production classifier has a camera-trap domain gap for
   photo-trained bird classes. It is near-perfect on eBird/iNat photos but 6
   to 22 points lower on real camera-trap snips of the same species, though
   it probably trained on many of them. Held-out camera-trap robins: 0.82,
   mostly lost to dusky robin.
2. This explains the judge's disagreement with Barry on synthetic
   scrubtits. It errs the same way (scrubtit called scrubwren) on real
   camera-trap scrubtits.
3. It is the clearest target for synthetic data: camera-trap-style snips of
   photo-dominated classes, which v2 already produces convincingly for
   robins.
4. Caution: the judge is more confident on synthetic camera-trap robins
   (0.98) than on real ones (0.91). That is consistent with synthetic snips
   being more prototypical (the diversity finding).
